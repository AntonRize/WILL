### Endnotes & Warnings

**Conflicts Between Sources:**

* **Earth's equatorial radius (Item 3):** The IAU 1964 system defined it as $6378.160$ km, which corresponds directly to the 1964 lunar sine parallax constant of $3422.451''$. The IAU 1976 system revised the radius down to $6378.140$ km.

**Missing Data (NOT FOUND):**

* **Item 4(a) Direct observational determination of Lunar Equation:** While the literature search located the specific primary citations for the direct measurement via asteroid radar/optical ranging (e.g., Delano, E. (1950) "The Lunar Equation From Observations of Eros, 1930-1931." *Astronomical Journal*, Vol. 55), the exact, unrounded numerical arcsecond values extracted by Delano and Rabe could not be retrieved in full before search constraints were exhausted.
* **Item 6 (Geodetic satellite orbit):** Primary orbit determination papers containing the unrounded semi-major axis and exact orbital period for LAGEOS-1 could not be retrieved within the search limits.
* **Item 7 (Fallback lunar orbiter):** Primary tracking data yielding exact orbital periods and radii for GRAIL, LRO, or Lunar Prospector could not be retrieved within the search limits.

**Circular Values to Avoid:**

* **Item 1 (Sine parallax):** The standard constant of sine parallax is explicitly defined as a geometric ratio of lengths ($\arcsin(a_e / r_m)$). Since $r_m$ (mean distance) is itself a Keplerian semi-major axis heavily dependent on the assumed GM of the Earth and Moon, this value is fully circular and computed.
* **Item 4(b) (Lunar inequality constant):** The official IAU value of $6.43987''$ was not an independent measurement; it was derived structurally from the adopted Earth/Moon mass ratio (81.30) multiplied by the solar parallax. It cannot be used as an independent geometric observable.
* **Item 5 (Solar parallax):** The IAU 1976 solar parallax ($8.794148''$) is a computed angle, directly derived from the predefined Earth radius and the radar-measured astronomical unit.
