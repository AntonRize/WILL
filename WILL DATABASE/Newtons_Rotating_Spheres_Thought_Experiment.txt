
\subsection{Newton's Rotating Spheres Thought Experiment}
\hypertarget{sec:Newtons_thought}{}\label{sec:Newtons_thought}

\subsubsection*{The Thought Experiment as Newton States It}

Two globes are kept at a given distance from one another by a cord that connects them, and are revolved about their common centre of gravity. From the tension of the cord we discover the endeavour of the globes to recede from the axis of their motion, and from that we compute the quantity of their circular motion. Newton states that this holds in an immense vacuum, where nothing external or sensible exists with which the globes could be compared.

Three features of this wording fix the problem. The cord is taut throughout, since the globes are kept at a given distance, so the separation is the cord length. The quantity to be computed is continuous, because Newton asks how much circular motion there is rather than whether there is any. The centre of rotation is named by Newton as the common centre of gravity, so it belongs to the given system.

The question is what the rotation is relative to, given that no external body has been listed.

\subsubsection*{Two Camps in Dispute}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Camp I (substantival):} The rotation is relative to space itself, which exists independently of matter and supplies the inertial structure. The tension is the endeavour of the globes to recede from an axis fixed in that structure. \textbf{Required primitive: the spacetime manifold.}

    \item \textit{Camp II (Machian):} The rotation is relative to the matter of the universe as a whole, and inertia is a relation among all masses. In a genuinely empty universe there is no such relation and therefore no tension. \textbf{Required primitive: the matter of the universe as a whole.}
\end{itemize}

Both camps accept Newton's framing, that a state of motion must be motion relative to something. They disagree only about the identity of that something. The dispute is a disagreement over which primitive to admit.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textit{Camp III ( Relational Geometry):} The third position admits no further primitive and reads the state from the participants Newton already listed.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Inventory]
\begin{itemize}
\item \(A\), \(B\): the two globes, each a physical body standing in a mutual relation of potential amplitude \(\kappa\);
\item \(d\): the cord, held at its given length, carrying potential amplitude \(\kappa_{\text{cord}}\);
\item \(C\): the common centre, located by light-time as the point equidistant from \(A\) and \(B\);
\item \(t_d\): light-time along the cord;
\item \(Z_{sys}\): the system shift of light emitted at \(A\) or \(B\) and received at \(C\).
\end{itemize}
No spacetime container, no external time axis, no distant matter, and no intrinsic mass are entered as primitives.
\end{definition}

Each globe stands in relation to \(C\) at distance \(d/2\), so the relational radius of the pair is \(a=d/2\). The cord holds both globes at fixed and equal radius, so the radial amplitude vanishes, the eccentricity is zero, and the kinematic amplitude is the same at every phase.

\subsubsection*{The Reading}

Light emitted at a globe and received at the centre carries both phases of the relation, because the globe is displaced from \(C\) on both carriers at once. The single observable is the product of the two shifts (\ref{sec:spectroscopic_shift}),
\begin{equation}
Z_{sys} \;=\; (1+z_\kappa)(1+z_\beta) \;=\; \frac{1}{\tau},
\qquad
\tau \;=\; \kappa_{X}\,\beta_{Y}.
\label{eq:globes-Zsys}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Two Channels, One Ledger}

The globes bind each other, and the cord binds them further. Both are potential relations and both are carried on \(S^{2}\). The relational ledger is allocated in quadratic form (\ref{sec:closure}), so each channel contributes its own squared amplitude and the contributions add:
\begin{equation}
\delta \;=\; \frac{\kappa^{2}+\kappa_{\text{cord}}^{2}}{2\beta^{2}} \;=\; 1.
\label{eq:globes-closure}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Share of the cord]
\hypertarget{thm:globes-cord}{}\label{thm:globes-cord}
The potential amplitude carried by the cord ($\kappa_{\text{cord}}^2$) is the part of the double kinematic amplitude ($2\beta^2$) that the mutual potential of the globes does not bind:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\text{cord}}^{2} \;=\; 2\beta^{2}-\kappa^{2}.
\label{eq:globes-cord}
\end{equation}
Three scenarios dictated by potential ($\kappa^2$) vs kinetic ($2\beta^2$) distribution:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $ 2\beta^{2}<\kappa^{2}$ --- in fall orbit and loos cord;
    \item $ 2\beta^{2}=\kappa^{2}$ --- stable circular orbit;
    \item $ 2\beta^{2}>\kappa^{2}$ --- escape orbit and taut cord.
\end{itemize}
So the excessive kinetic energy that would turn stable circular orbit in to escape one is the tension energy that the cord bind $\kappa_{\text{cord}}^{2} $. 
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The pair is relationally closed (\ref{thm:relational_closure}), so every channel acting on it belongs to its ledger and the closure factor is unity (\ref{def:closure_factor}). Constancy of the cord length gives fixed and equal radius for both globes, hence \(e=0\) and \(\beta_{R}=0\), so the kinematic amplitude is the global invariant \(\beta\). Degree-of-freedom indifference (\ref{lem:dof-indifference}) assigns equal quadratic weight to each independent degree of freedom, so the total potential amplitude $(\kappa_{\text{total}}^2=\kappa^2+\kappa_{\text{cord}}^2)$ of the closed pair is equal to \(2\beta^{2}\) and each channel enters through its own square. Rearranging \eqref{eq:globes-closure} isolates the cord's share. Unequal weighting of the two channels, or any addition law other than the quadratic one, would violate the DOF Indifference Lemma (\ref{lem:dof-indifference}).
\end{proof}

The causal order runs from the kinematic amplitude ($\beta$) to the cord. The cord does not generate the state. The globes carry an amplitude \(\beta\) that the mutual potential ($\kappa$) alone cannot bind, and the cord holds the remainder. When \(\kappa^{2}=2\beta^{2}\) the mutual potential binds the whole kinetic amplitude, the cord carries nothing, and the pair is a stable circular orbit. Newton's cord is loaded, so \(\kappa^{2}<2\beta^{2}\).

\subsubsection*{Newton's Question Answered}

Newton asks for the quantity of the circular motion. The complementary phases of the closed pair are
\begin{equation}
\beta_{Y}=\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}},
\qquad
\kappa_{X}=\sqrt{1-2\beta^{2}},
\qquad
\beta^{2}<\tfrac{1}{2},
\label{eq:globes-phases}
\end{equation}
so the single observable \eqref{eq:globes-Zsys} becomes
\begin{equation}
\tau^{2} \;=\; \left(1-2\beta^{2}\right)\left(1-\beta^{2}\right).
\label{eq:globes-tau}
\end{equation}
Inverting for the amplitude gives the quantity Newton asked for, from one optical reading taken inside the system:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\beta^{2} \;=\; \frac{1}{4}\left(3-\sqrt{1+8\tau^{2}}\right)\;}
\label{eq:globes-answer}
\end{equation}

The scale of the relation follows from the double kinetic projection and orbital radius:
\begin{equation}
R_{s} \;=\; 2\beta^{2}a \;=\; \beta^{2}d
\label{eq:globes-scale}
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Optional period]
The globes co-rotate with the cord, so their mutual arrangement never changes and a period counted against the sky is not a reading of this inventory. If a period is wanted for comparison with a clock convention it is a derived label,
\begin{equation}
T \;=\; \frac{\pi t_d}{\beta},
\qquad
N \;=\; \frac{T}{t_d} \;=\; \frac{\pi}{\beta},
\label{eq:globes-period}
\end{equation}
where \(N\) is the number of cord light-crossings per derived revolution.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Settlement of the Thought Experiment}

\begin{theorem}[No extra primitive is required]
\hypertarget{thm:globes-settlement}{}\label{thm:globes-settlement}
The quantity of circular motion Newton asks for is \(\beta^{2}\), obtained from the system shift received at the common centre. The load in the cord is \(\kappa_{\text{cord}}^{2}=2\beta^{2}-\kappa^{2}\), the share of the ledger the mutual relation of the globes leaves unbound. The question of what the rotation is relative to does not arise. Camp~I's spacetime manifold and Camp~II's cosmic matter are surplus to the computation Newton himself demanded.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every symbol in \eqref{eq:globes-Zsys} through \eqref{eq:globes-scale} is either a listed participant, namely the two globes, the cord and its centre, or a reading exchanged among them, namely \(t_d\) and \(Z_{sys}\). The relational radius is the cord length halved. The amplitudes \(\beta\), \(\kappa\) and \(\kappa_{\text{cord}}\) are projections of one closed ledger, tied by degree-of-freedom indifference alone. No further relatum is quantified over at any step. Newton's own demand, that the quantity be obtainable in an immense vacuum with nothing external to compare against, is met by construction, because nothing external appears in the inventory.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Reductio ad Absurdum of the Classical Tension}

The classical expression for the tension is recovered by appending posits to \eqref{eq:globes-cord}, in the same manner as the Minkowski and Schwarzschild intervals, the Lagrangian, the Hamiltonian and Newton's third law (\ref{rem:absurdum}).

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Intrinsic mass.} Each globe is granted a scalar \(m\) belonging to it in isolation.
\item \textbf{Force as a primitive.} A force is posited and related to motion by \(F=ma\).
\item \textbf{An inertial frame.} Acceleration is defined as a second derivative of position, which requires a frame in which the globes accelerate.
\item \textbf{Externally flowing time.} An angular rate \(\omega=d\theta/dt\) is posited against an autonomous coordinate time.
\item \textbf{A coordinate grid.} The motion is resolved into radial and transverse components against imposed axes.
\end{enumerate}

Under these posits the derived period \eqref{eq:globes-period} becomes an angular rate \(\omega=2\beta c/d\), each globe is assigned a centripetal acceleration at radius \(d/2\), and the cord is assigned the force that supplies whatever the mutual attraction does not:
\begin{equation}
T_{\text{ension}} \;=\; m\,\omega^{2}\,\frac{d}{2}-\frac{G m^{2}}{d^{2}} \;=\; \frac{E_{0}}{d}\left(2\beta^{2}-\kappa^{2}\right) \;=\; \frac{E_{0}\,\kappa_{\text{cord}}^{2}}{d}.
\label{eq:globes-tension}
\end{equation}
The tension is the cord's share of the ledger wearing a mass label and a force label.

The classical route presupposes what Newton's argument concludes. The angular rate is measured against a reference direction, and an immense vacuum supplies none. The substantival camp has one escape and it closes on them: they may define the angular rate by the tension itself, in which case they has no independent handle on it and the tension is a relabelling of the amplitude the relation already carries.

The forced choice between background space and the matter of the universe as a whole, is surplus.
