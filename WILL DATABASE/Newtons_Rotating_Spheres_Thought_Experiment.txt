
\subsection{Newton's Rotating Spheres Thought Experiment}
\hypertarget{sec:Newtons_thought}{}\label{sec:Newtons_thought}

\subsubsection*{The thought experiment}

Two spheres \(A\) and \(B\) are joined by a cord and placed in an otherwise empty universe. Two configurations are compared. In one, the pair does not rotate and the cord is slack. In the other, the pair rotates about its common centre and the cord is under tension. The two states are physically distinct. The question is what, if anything, the rotation is relative to, given that no external body has been listed.

Write \(d\) for the length of the cord and \(D\) for the operational separation of the two spheres.
Slack means \(d>D\). Tension means the constraint is active: \(d=D\).

\subsubsection*{\textbf{Two camps in dispute}}

\textit{Camp I (substantival / absolute inertial structure).}
The rotation is relative to space itself, which exists independently of matter and supplies the inertial structure. The taut state is distinguished by angular motion with respect to that structure. 
In modern language: the pair has nonzero angular velocity relative to a background absolute space, or a substantival spacetime manifold. 
\textbf{Required primitive: spacetime manifold.}

\textit{Camp II (Machian / cosmic matter).}
The rotation is relative to the matter of the universe as a whole. Inertia is a relation among all masses.
In a genuinely empty universe there is no such relation, so there is no taut state. The distinction Newton draws can appear only when the matter of the rest of the Universe included. 
\textbf{Required primitive: matter of the universe as a whole.}

The logical conflict is a dependence claim.
Camp~I holds that the distinguishing quantity survives in the limit where all other matter is removed.
Camp~II holds that it vanishes in that limit. Both camps accept Newton's framing: if the two states differ, the difference is a motion \emph{relative to something}. They disagree only about the identity of that something. 
The disagreement is on which fundamental primitive required.
