\section{Derivation of the Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation}

\begin{equation}

\subsection{Clean-Sample Verification: Methodology and Results}
\hypertarget{sec:overtone_clean}{}\label{sec:overtone_clean}

\paragraph{Motivation.}
The two distances compared in this test do not have the same exposure to data quality. The catalogue distance $D_{SPARC}$ is obtained by an independent method (Hubble flow, tip of the red giant branch, Cepheids, cluster membership or supernovae) and is unaffected by the quality of the rotation curve or of the photometric decomposition. The Overtone Scale clean distance $D_{RG}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:galactic_overtone_distance}) is built entirely from these two data streams. A galaxy whose tabulated profiles are physically inconsistent therefore corrupts $D_{RG}$ but not $D_{SPARC}$, and a comparison on the full catalogue would measure data defects as much as the relational algorithm. By \href{https://willrg.com/documents/WILL_RG_I.pdf#pr:epistemic}{Epistemic Hygiene}, the algorithm is tested only on galaxies whose data satisfy explicit physical-consistency criteria, fixed before any distance was computed. None of the criteria uses $V_{obs}$ against a distance, or $D_{SPARC}$ itself, so the selection cannot be tuned toward agreement.

\paragraph{Global scale.}
The only global input is the structural resonance scale, computed from the inputs of Section~\ref{sec:deriving-H0} ($T_0=2.7255\,\mathrm{K}$, $\alpha=7.29735\times10^{-3}$, $G=6.674\times10^{-11}$, $\sigma_{SB}=5.67037\times10^{-8}$, $c=299\,792\,458\,\mathrm{m/s}$):
\begin{equation}
\rho_\gamma=\frac{4\sigma_{SB}T_0^4}{c^3},\qquad \rho_{max}=\frac{\rho_\gamma}{3\alpha^2},\qquad H_0=\sqrt{8\pi G\rho_{max}},\qquad a_\kappa=\frac{cH_0}{3\pi},\qquad 3\pi R_H=\frac{c^2}{a_\kappa},
\end{equation}
giving $H_0=68.1456\,\mathrm{km/s/Mpc}$ and $a_\kappa=7.0248\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. No quantity is adjusted per galaxy.

\paragraph{Algorithm.}
Let $i$ number the tabulated points of a rotation curve, $i=1$ being the innermost. From the SPARC columns (radius $Rad$ in kpc, $D_{SPARC}$ in Mpc, velocities in km/s):
\begin{align}
\theta_i &= \frac{Rad_i}{D_{SPARC}}, \qquad \beta_{obs,i}=\frac{V_{obs,i}}{c},\\
V_{bar,i}^2 &= \operatorname{sign}(V_{gas,i})\,V_{gas,i}^2+\Upsilon_{disk}V_{disk,i}^2+\Upsilon_{bulge}V_{bulge,i}^2,\qquad \Upsilon_{disk}=0.5,\ \Upsilon_{bulge}=0.7,\\
g_{bar,i} &= \frac{V_{bar,i}^2}{Rad_i},\qquad e_{0,i}=\sqrt{\frac{a_\kappa}{g_{bar,i}}},\\
D(\theta_i) &= \frac{3\pi R_H\,\beta_{obs,i}^2}{\theta_i}\cdot\frac{e_{0,i}^2}{1+e_{0,i}}.
\end{align}
The distance of a galaxy, $D_{RG}$, is the median of $D(\theta_i)$ over all points with $e_{0,i}\ge e_{bull}$, $e_{bull}=1$, i.e.\ over the Tone-dominated profile beyond the Equivalence Point of Section~\ref{sec:baryonic_escape}.

SPARC tabulates $V_{gas}$ with its sign: a negative value is the outward pull of a gas ring lying outside $r$ (a central HI depression), and it enters $V_{bar}^2$ negatively. 

\paragraph{Independence from the catalogue distance and from its $H_0$.}
SPARC computed $Rad$ and each of $V_{gas}^2$, $V_{disk}^2$, $V_{bulge}^2$ at the assumed distance $D_{SPARC}$, and each is proportional to it; $V_{obs}$ is a Doppler measurement and is not. Consequently $\theta_i$ and $g_{bar,i}$, hence $e_{0,i}$ and $D(\theta_i)$, contain no trace of $D_{SPARC}$. In particular, for Hubble-flow galaxies ($\mathrm{f\_Dist}=1$), whose $D_{SPARC}$ was built with $H_0=73\,\mathrm{km/s/Mpc}$, the value $73$ cancels from every data stream entering $D_{RG}$. It affects only the comparison target, which is therefore rescaled to $D_{SPARC}\times 73/H_0 = D_{SPARC}\times1.07124$ (and its quoted uncertainty $e_D$ likewise). Numerically, doubling $D_{SPARC}$ together with every column proportional to it returns the same $D_{RG}$ to better than $10^{-9}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.

\paragraph{Selection criteria.}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Physical values.} Remove a galaxy if $V_{obs}\le0$ or $V_{bar}^2\le0$ at any point. A non-positive observed speed or a non-positive baryonic share has no admissible projection on $S^1$ or $S^2$, and $e_0$ is undefined.
    \item \textbf{No falling start.} Remove a galaxy if the mean $V_{obs}$ of its first three points exceeds the mean of its last three. Such profiles are dominated by unresolved central structure or truncated outer data and do not sample the Tone-dominated regime.
    \item \textbf{Monotonic Tone eccentricity beyond the Equivalence Point.} Starting from the first point with $e_0\ge1$, require $e_{0,n}<e_{0,n+1}$ at every subsequent point; a galaxy never reaching $e_0=1$ is removed. From $\beta_{bar}^2=R_s/2r$ and $g_{bar}=c^2\beta_{bar}^2/r$,
    \begin{equation}
    e_0^2=\frac{2\,a_\kappa\,r^2}{c^2\,R_s(r)},
    \end{equation}
    so $e_0$ decreases between two points only when the enclosed horizon scale $R_s(r)$ grows faster than $r^2$, i.e.\ when the annulus between them carries more baryons than the mean interior. A smooth exponential stellar disk of scale length $R_{disk}$ has $g_{bar}$ decreasing at every radius beyond $0.75\,R_{disk}$, so past the Equivalence Point it produces a strictly rising $e_0$. A reversal therefore signals either unmodelled structure (HI rings, spiral arms, bars) or noise in the decomposition; SPARC provides no uncertainties on $V_{gas}$, $V_{disk}$ or $V_{bulge}$, so the two cannot be separated point by point. The criterion restricts the test to galaxies whose baryonic budget has converged smoothly, the regime in which $e_0=r/R_{trans}$ holds literally. It uses only the photometric and HI columns.
    \item \textbf{Sampling of the Tone-dominated profile.} Require at least 10 points with $e_0\ge1$, i.e.\ at least 10 points actually used in $D_{RG}$.
    \item \textbf{Rotation-curve quality.} Require SPARC $\mathrm{Qual}\le2$, excluding curves with major asymmetries or strong non-circular motions.
    \item \textbf{Inclination.} Require $i\ge30^\circ$; $V_{obs}$ is deprojected by $1/\sin i$ and $D_{RG}\propto V_{obs}^2$, so low inclinations amplify inclination errors into the distance.
\end{enumerate}
